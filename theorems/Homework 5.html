Problem # 1. (Exercise 1.4.1a) Let H⁢(t,x) be the heat kernel on (0,∞)×ℝn. Define its extension to ℝ×ℝn by

    Solution.

    For η>0, define

    Hη⁢(x,t)={He:t>η0:t≤η
    1. (i)

      Hη→He in 𝒟′⁢(ℝ×ℝn), as η→0.

      So, Hη→He in as η→0.

    2. (ii)

      (∂t−Δ)⁢Hη→δ in 𝒟′⁢(ℝ×ℝn) as η→0.

      For ϕ∈𝒟⁢(ℝ×ℝn), we have

      ⟨(∂t−Δ)⁢Hη,ϕ⟩ =⟨Hη,(−∂t−Δ)⁢ϕ⟩=∫η∞∫ℝnHe⁢(−∂t−Δ)⁢ϕ⁢𝑑x⁢𝑑t (3)
      =∫ℝnH⁢(η,x)⁢ϕ⁢(η,x)⁢𝑑x+∫η∞∫ℝn(∂t−Δ)⁢H⁢ϕ⁢𝑑x⁢𝑑t

      Since H is an approximate identity, we have that

      limη→0⟨(∂t−Δ)⁢Hη,ϕ⟩=ϕ⁢(0)=⟨δ,ϕ⟩ (4)

      Since this holds for all such ϕ, we have that limη→0(∂t−Δ)⁢Hη=δ in 𝒟′⁢(ℝ×ℝn).

      Clearly, (∂t−Δ) is a differential operator with smooth coefficients. Therefore, (∂t−Δ):𝒟′⁢(ℝ×ℝn)→𝒟′⁢(ℝ×ℝn) is continuous, and we have

      δ=limη→0(∂t−Δ)⁢Hη=(∂t−Δ)⁢(limη→0Hη)=(∂t−Δ)⁢He (5)
    3. (iii)

      Where does your argument use the fact that the topology of 𝒟′⁢(ℝ×ℝn) is Hausdorff?

      We used Hausdorff(ness) in (5). We have that limη→0(∂t−Δ)⁢Hη=δ, and we have that limη→0(∂t−Δ)⁢Hη=(∂t−Δ)⁢He. We use the fact that limits are unique to conclude that δ=(∂t−Δ)⁢He.

    ∎
